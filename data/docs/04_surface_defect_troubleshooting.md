# Steel Surface Defect Troubleshooting Guide

Six defect classes are detected by the visual inspection model. Each has a
different root cause and a different corrective action.

## Crazing (Cr)
Fine network of shallow cracks across the strip surface, often described as a
spider-web or alligator pattern.
- **Causes:** rolling temperature too low; excessive reduction in a single
  pass; surface chilling from uneven cooling spray.
- **Actions:** raise the finishing temperature; redistribute reduction across
  more passes; check the cooling header for blocked nozzles.
- **Severity:** high — crazed material usually cannot be reworked and is scrapped.

## Inclusion (In)
Foreign non-metallic material embedded in the steel surface, appearing as dark
streaks or spots elongated in the rolling direction.
- **Causes:** slag or refractory carryover from the ladle; poor deoxidation
  practice; contaminated scrap charge.
- **Actions:** review ladle cleanliness and slag-carryover practice at the
  caster; check tundish refractory condition; audit incoming scrap quality.
- **Severity:** high — inclusions are internal and will not polish out.

## Patches (Pa)
Irregular discoloured areas of varying size with diffuse edges.
- **Causes:** uneven descaling; residual oxide left before rolling; uneven
  emulsion coverage on the mill.
- **Actions:** verify descaler spray pressure and nozzle pattern; check
  emulsion flow and concentration; inspect work-roll surface condition.
- **Severity:** medium — often cosmetic, but the customer specification decides.

## Pitted Surface (PS)
Clusters of small cavities or pinholes distributed over the surface.
- **Causes:** acid attack during pickling; over-pickling from excessive line
  time; corrosion of coils stored in humid conditions.
- **Actions:** reduce pickling line time or acid concentration; verify the
  inhibitor dose; move coil storage to a dry, covered area.
- **Severity:** medium to high — depends on pit depth relative to tolerance.

## Rolled-in Scale (RS)
Oxide scale pressed into the surface during rolling, showing as dark irregular
patches with hard edges.
- **Causes:** incomplete descaling before the finishing stand; furnace
  atmosphere too oxidising; long delay between reheat and rolling.
- **Actions:** increase descaler pressure; reduce furnace residence time or
  adjust the air-to-fuel ratio; shorten the delay between reheat and rolling.
- **Severity:** high — rolled-in scale is embedded and cannot be removed later.

## Scratches (Sc)
Straight or curved linear marks, usually running along the handling direction.
- **Causes:** damaged roller table or guide; foreign debris on the line;
  mishandling during coiling, transport or storage.
- **Actions:** inspect and replace worn guides and roller bearings; clean
  debris from the line; review coil handling and crane practice.
- **Severity:** low to medium — shallow scratches may be acceptable depending
  on the end use.

## Escalation rule
If the same defect class appears on more than three consecutive coils, stop the
line and raise a process engineering investigation rather than continuing to
inspect and sort.
