# Tool Wear Failure (TWF) — Maintenance Guide

## What it is
Tool wear failure occurs when the cutting tool degrades past its usable life.
In the AI4I process, tools are replaced or fail somewhere between 200 and 240
minutes of accumulated tool wear. Beyond this window the tool no longer holds
dimensional tolerance and either the tool is swapped out preventively or it
fails in service.

## Warning signs
- Tool wear reading above 200 minutes
- Gradual rise in torque at constant rotational speed (a dull tool needs more force)
- Surface finish deteriorating on inspected parts
- Increase in scrap or rework rate on the line

## Root causes
- Running the tool past its rated life to avoid a changeover stop
- Incorrect cutting speed or feed rate for the material
- Insufficient or interrupted coolant flow
- Abrasive workpiece material, common with the L (low quality) product variant
- Worn spindle bearings causing vibration that chips the cutting edge

## Recommended actions
1. Stop the machine at the next available break when wear exceeds 200 minutes.
2. Inspect the cutting edge under magnification for flank wear and chipping.
3. Replace the tool insert. Record the actual wear-at-replacement value.
4. Reset the tool wear counter in the machine controller.
5. Verify the first three parts after changeover against the drawing.

## Preventive measures
- Set a hard replacement threshold at 200 minutes of tool wear.
- Schedule tool changes to coincide with planned shift breaks.
- Keep at least two spare inserts per tool at the machine.
- Track wear-at-failure per product variant; the L variant typically wears
  tooling faster than the H variant.

## Typical downtime
Planned tool change: 10–15 minutes. Unplanned tool failure: 45–90 minutes,
plus scrapped parts from the affected batch.
