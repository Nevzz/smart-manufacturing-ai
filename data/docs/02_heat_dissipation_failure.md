# Heat Dissipation Failure (HDF) — Maintenance Guide

## What it is
Heat dissipation failure happens when the machine cannot shed process heat fast
enough. The condition is defined by two things occurring together: the
difference between process temperature and air temperature drops below about
8.6 K, and rotational speed is under roughly 1380 rpm. With too little
temperature gradient and too little airflow from the spindle, heat accumulates
in the cutting zone.

## Warning signs
- Temperature difference (process minus air) falling below 9 K
- Rotational speed sustained under 1400 rpm
- Ambient shop temperature climbing above normal, especially in summer
- Coolant returning warm to the tank
- Thermal discolouration on finished parts

## Root causes
- Blocked or dust-clogged cooling fins and air intakes
- Coolant pump running below rated flow, or a partially blocked nozzle
- Low coolant level or degraded coolant concentration
- Ambient plant temperature too high; inadequate ventilation near the machine
- Running a heavy cut at low spindle speed, which generates heat without airflow

## Recommended actions
1. Check coolant level and top up to the marked line.
2. Measure coolant concentration with a refractometer; correct if outside spec.
3. Clean the heat exchanger fins and air intake filters with compressed air.
4. Verify the coolant pump pressure against the nameplate rating.
5. If temperature difference stays below 8.6 K after the above, stop the machine
   and inspect the chiller unit.

## Preventive measures
- Clean intake filters weekly; clean the heat exchanger monthly.
- Log ambient and process temperature at the start of every shift.
- Avoid sustained operation below 1400 rpm under heavy load.
- Install shop-floor ventilation or spot cooling where ambient regularly
  exceeds 302 K.

## Typical downtime
Filter cleaning: 20 minutes. Coolant system service: 2 hours. Chiller
replacement: 1 shift or more.
